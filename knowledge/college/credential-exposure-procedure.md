## About this procedure

Sample procedure written for a course project. It is not an official policy of {{COLLEGE_NAME}}. It applies when a student typed or entered their college username and password into a login page that may have been fake, for example after clicking a link in an email or text about their college account. In security terms this is a credential exposure. It does not require proof that the page was malicious: if there is reasonable doubt, follow it.

## Priority levels

- Password entered only: high priority. Act within the hour.
- Password entered and an MFA code given or a sign-in prompt approved: urgent. The attacker may already be signed in. Act immediately and contact {{HELPDESK}} at the same time.

## Steps for students

1. Change your college password now. Type the official portal address ending in {{COLLEGE_DOMAIN}} yourself; never use a link from the suspicious message.
2. If you cannot sign in because the password was already changed, do not keep trying: contact {{HELPDESK}} for account recovery.
3. Sign out of all other sessions from the account's security settings, if available.
4. Check your registered MFA methods and remove anything you did not add.
5. In your college mailbox, check for forwarding rules, filters or auto-replies you did not create.
6. If you used the same or a similar password for any other account, change it there too, starting with your personal email.
7. Report to {{HELPDESK}}, including what you entered, when, and what you have already done.

## What the helpdesk needs to know

The time of the incident, the suspicious message or link, whether an MFA code was entered or a prompt approved, whether the password is reused elsewhere, and which steps you have completed. Separate what you know happened from what you are unsure about.
