## About this procedure

Sample procedure written for a course project. It is not an official policy of {{COLLEGE_NAME}}. It applies when a student has received a suspicious message about their college account but has not clicked any link, opened any attachment or entered any information.

## What the college will never ask by message

{{COLLEGE_NAME}} IT staff will never ask you by email, SMS, WhatsApp or phone for your password, for an MFA code, or to approve a sign-in request. College systems are reached through addresses ending in {{COLLEGE_DOMAIN}}. A message that asks for any of these, or links to a sign-in page on a different domain, should be treated as phishing.

## Steps for students

1. Do not click links, scan QR codes, open attachments or reply.
2. If the message mentions a problem with your account, check it yourself: type the college portal address ending in {{COLLEGE_DOMAIN}} into your browser and sign in normally. A real problem will be visible there.
3. Report the message to {{HELPDESK}}, using the report button in your email client if available, or by forwarding the message as an attachment.
4. Delete the message after reporting.
5. Do not forward it to other students as a warning; tell them about it instead.

## When to escalate

If you notice that classmates received the same message, mention it in your report. If the message seems to come from a real college staff member's account, tell {{HELPDESK}} so they can check whether that account was compromised.
