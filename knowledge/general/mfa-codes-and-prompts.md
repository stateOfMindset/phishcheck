## Why MFA matters

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) means that a password alone is not enough to sign in: you also need a code from an app or text message, a push approval on your phone, or a security key. It stops most attacks that rely on a stolen password, which is why attackers try to trick people into handing over the second factor as well.

## If you entered an MFA code on a phishing page

Treat this as urgent. Modern phishing pages pass your password and code to the real service in real time, so the attacker may already be signed in to your account. Change your password immediately from a trusted device, sign out all other sessions, review your MFA methods for anything you did not add, and report it to the account's helpdesk straight away so they can end the attacker's session.

## If you approved a sign-in request you did not start

Approving an unexpected push notification gives the attacker the same access as entering a code. Follow the same urgent steps: change the password, sign out all sessions, check MFA methods and report immediately.

## MFA fatigue (prompt bombing)

Some attackers who already have your password send many sign-in prompts in a row, sometimes at night, hoping you approve one just to make them stop, or message you pretending to be IT support asking you to approve "a test". Never approve a prompt you did not start. Repeated unexpected prompts mean someone has your password: deny them, change your password, and report it.

## Codes requested by message or phone

No legitimate organisation will ask you to read out, type in or forward a one-time code you received. A code is meant only for the sign-in you are doing yourself. Anyone asking for it, including someone who claims to be from IT support or the bank, is trying to take over your account.

## Making MFA harder to abuse

Use an authenticator app or a security key where possible, rather than codes by text message. If your service shows a number to match or the location of a sign-in request, check it before approving. Keep at least one backup method registered so that you can recover your account if you lose your phone.
