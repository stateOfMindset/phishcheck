title: How to check a suspicious message independently
source_type: general

## The core rule

Never use the contact details, links, phone numbers or QR codes inside a suspicious message to check whether that same message is real. If the message is fake, all of those lead to the attacker. Verify through a channel you found yourself and already trust.

## Ways to verify

- Open the organisation's website or app yourself, by typing the address you know or using a saved bookmark, and sign in there. If there really is a problem with your account, you will see it after signing in normally.
- Call the organisation using a number from its official website, the back of your card, or an earlier bill, not the number in the message.
- For a message that seems to come from someone you know, contact that person on a different channel (call them, or message them on another app) and ask whether they sent it.
- For messages about your college account, sign in to the official college portal directly or ask the college IT helpdesk.

## What not to do while checking

Do not reply to the message to ask whether it is genuine; the attacker will simply say yes. Do not forward it to classmates or colleagues to ask their opinion, because one of them may click the link. Do not call a phone number given in the message. Do not scan a QR code from the message.

## When verification is not possible

If you cannot verify a message quickly, it is safe to ignore it and report it. A real organisation will still be able to reach you, and anything genuinely urgent will appear when you sign in to your account through the normal route.

## Limits of automated checks

Tools that inspect a link or an email's technical headers can reveal warning signs, but they cannot prove a message is safe. A message can pass technical checks and still be a scam, for example when it comes from a real account that was hacked, or from a lookalike domain the attacker owns. Independent verification is the only reliable confirmation.
