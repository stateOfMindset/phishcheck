## Risky file types

Attachments are a common way to install malware. Be especially careful with programs and scripts (.exe, .msi, .bat, .cmd, .js, .vbs, .scr), disk images and archives (.iso, .img, .zip, .rar, .7z, particularly password-protected ones), web pages sent as files (.html, .htm), and office documents that ask you to "enable content", "enable editing" or "enable macros". An invoice, CV, delivery note or "shared document" you were not expecting is a typical disguise.

## If you received but did not open it

Do not open it and do not preview it. Report the message and delete it. If you think it might be genuine, contact the sender through a channel you trust and ask.

## If you opened it

1. If anything ran, installed, or asked to enable content and you agreed, disconnect the device from the network (turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the cable) to limit what the malware can do.
2. Do not sign in to sensitive accounts, such as banking or email, from that device until it has been checked.
3. Run a full scan with your antivirus or the built-in security tool.
4. Report it to your IT helpdesk, saying which file you opened, when, and what happened on screen.
5. From a different, trusted device, change the passwords of accounts you used on the affected device.

## If it was a web page attachment

An .html attachment usually opens a fake sign-in page locally in your browser. If you typed a password into it, follow the guidance for entering a password on a phishing page.

## What not to do

Do not forward the attachment to anyone to "check" it, and do not upload it to random online scanners if it may contain personal or confidential information. Leave the analysis to your IT or security team.
