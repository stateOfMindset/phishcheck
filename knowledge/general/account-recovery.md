## Signs an account has been taken over

You cannot sign in with your usual password; you receive password-reset, sign-in or MFA messages you did not trigger; contacts receive messages from you that you did not send; settings such as the recovery phone or email have changed; or you see sign-ins from unknown devices or locations.

## Recovering access

Use the service's official account recovery process, reached by typing the website address yourself or through the official app, never through a link in an email or text. Recovery usually relies on a backup email address, a phone number or recovery codes. If those were changed by the attacker, contact the service's support or, for a work or college account, the IT helpdesk, who can verify your identity in other ways.

## Securing the account once you are back in

1. Set a new, unique password.
2. Sign out of all other sessions and devices.
3. Remove MFA methods, devices, recovery options and connected apps that are not yours, then make sure MFA is turned on.
4. Check email forwarding rules, filters and auto-replies, and remove any you did not create.
5. Look through recent activity and sent messages so you can warn people who may have received messages from the attacker.

## Start with your email account

Your main email account is the key to your other accounts, because password resets are sent there. If several accounts are affected, secure your email first, then work through the others, starting with those that hold money, personal data or access to other systems.

## Preventing it next time

Use a different password for every important account (a password manager helps), turn on MFA wherever possible, keep your recovery email and phone number up to date, and keep devices and browsers updated.
