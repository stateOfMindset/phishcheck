## Why reporting matters

Reporting helps even when you did nothing wrong. Security teams use reports to block the sender and the link for everyone, to find out who else received the same message, and to take down the fake website. The sooner they hear about it, the fewer people are affected.

## How to report

- In most email services there is a "Report phishing" or "Report" button. Use it, because it sends the full message including its technical headers.
- For a work or college account, also follow the organisation's own reporting route, usually the IT helpdesk.
- If there is no report button, forward the message as an attachment rather than inline, so the original headers are kept.
- For scam text messages, many mobile carriers and national authorities accept reports by forwarding the message; check your country's official guidance.

## What to include

A useful report says what you received and what you did, kept separate from guesses. Include:

- When the message arrived and on which account or device.
- The sender address, subject line, and any links or attachments (do not click them again to copy them).
- Exactly what you did: only read it, clicked the link, downloaded or opened a file, entered a password, entered an MFA code or approved a prompt.
- Whether that password is used for any other account.
- What you have already done, such as changing the password.
- Anything you are not sure about.

## After reporting

Delete the message once it has been reported, unless your helpdesk asks you to keep it. Do not forward it to friends or classmates as a warning, because someone may click it; describe it to them instead, or let the helpdesk send an official warning.
